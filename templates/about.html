Research

About TruthScan

Honours research project — Bachelor of ICT, Durban University of Technology, 2026.

The Research Problem

Fake news spreads faster than corrections. Existing detection systems struggle to generalise across different news sources and domains. This project addresses the cross-domain generalisation gap in automated fake news detection.

The Solution

A three-model stacking ensemble that combines the strengths of transformer-based contextual understanding, sequential pattern learning and gradient-boosted feature classification. The ensemble was trained on 116,971 articles from two independent datasets.

Architecture

BERT bert-base-uncased fine-tuned for 3 epochs. Extracts 768-dimensional CLS embeddings capturing deep contextual language understanding. 98.28% F1
BiLSTM Two-layer bidirectional LSTM with 128 hidden units per direction and 0.3 dropout. Learns sequential patterns from BERT embeddings. 98.68% F1
XGBoost Gradient boosted trees on TF-IDF features (50,000 unigrams and bigrams). Fast, interpretable and robust to noise. 96.60% F1
Ensemble XGBoost meta-learner combining all three models. 50,832 stacked features. 5-fold cross-validation. McNemar's p = 0.000012. 99.94% F1

Datasets

WELFake

72,134 articles. Balanced: 37,106 real and 35,028 fake. Multi-source news content from the internet.

ISOT

44,898 articles. Real news from Reuters. Fake news from unreliable sources. University of Victoria.

Key Finding

The stacking ensemble achieved 99.88% F1 on the out-of-domain ISOT dataset, an F1 degradation of −0.0121 compared to WELFake. The research hypothesis that F1 degradation would be below 5% was confirmed with statistical significance at p < 0.0001.
